# Presentation Script
## A/B Test Analysis: Should We Roll Out the Stronger Boost?
**Target: 6-8 minutes**

---

## SLIDE 1: Title (30 seconds)

> "Today I'll be presenting the results of our A/B test analysis, answering a critical question for our game: Should we roll out the stronger boost to all players?
>
> I'll walk you through the problem, how we tested it, what we found, and my recommendation."

---

## SLIDE 2: The Problem (45 seconds)

> "Let's start with why we ran this test in the first place.
>
> We received player feedback that some levels were too hard. This frustration was leading to potential player churn - players quitting the game.
>
> Our hypothesis was simple: if we make the boost power-up stronger, players will succeed more often, have a better experience, and stay engaged - without breaking the game's balance.
>
> So we designed an A/B test to find out."

---

## SLIDE 3: The Experiment (1 minute)

> "Here's how we set up the experiment.
>
> We split our players into two groups:
> - Group A received the stronger boost
> - Group B kept the original boost as our control
>
> The test ran for 4 months, from June through September 2020. Before that, we had 5 months of baseline data, and after the test ended in October, we reset everyone to the original boost to see if effects persisted.
>
> Our sample size was robust: about 14,000 players across both groups, generating 1.5 million level attempts during the test period.
>
> This gives us strong statistical power to detect meaningful differences."

---

## SLIDE 4: The Result (1 minute)

> "Now for our primary finding - and it's significant.
>
> Players with the stronger boost achieved a 2.6 percentage point higher success rate. Group A succeeded 25.1% of the time, compared to 22.5% for Group B.
>
> Critically, this difference is statistically significant with a p-value less than 0.001. This means there's less than a 0.1% chance this difference is due to random variation.
>
> As you can see in the chart, the difference is clear and consistent."

---

## SLIDE 5: The Effect Over Time (45 seconds)

> "Looking at the time series, we can see the story unfold.
>
> Before the test, both groups performed similarly - this validates our randomization.
>
> During the test period, there's a clear separation, with Group A consistently outperforming Group B.
>
> But here's the surprising part: even after we reset the boost in October, Group A continued to outperform. The effect persisted.
>
> This suggests players developed better strategies or gained confidence during the test that carried forward."

---

## SLIDE 6: Players Love It (45 seconds)

> "Beyond success rates, we also measured engagement with the boost feature itself.
>
> Players in Group A used the boost 51% more often than Group B - 15.6% versus 10.3% usage rate.
>
> This is important because it shows players recognized the value of the stronger boost and actively chose to use it more.
>
> They weren't just passively benefiting - they were engaging more with the feature. That's a strong signal of improved player experience."

---

## SLIDE 7: The Full Picture (1 minute)

> "Let me show you the complete before, during, and after picture.
>
> Pre-test: Both groups were similar, with about a 1 percentage point difference - this small baseline difference is something we'll acknowledge as a limitation.
>
> During the test: Group A jumped ahead by 2.6 percentage points.
>
> Post-test - and this is key: Even after we reset everyone to the original boost power, Group A maintained that same 2.6 percentage point advantage.
>
> This persistence effect suggests habit formation or learned behavior. Players who experienced success with the stronger boost developed skills or confidence that lasted beyond the test period."

---

## SLIDE 8: The Trade-offs (1 minute)

> "Before making a recommendation, let's consider both sides.
>
> Arguments FOR rolling out:
> - We have a proven 2.6% improvement in success rate
> - It's statistically significant beyond doubt
> - Players engage 51% more with the feature
> - And the positive effects last even after the test
>
> Arguments AGAINST:
> - The easier success might reduce long-term challenge
> - Players could become dependent on boosts
> - We haven't fully analyzed monetization impact
> - And there was a small baseline difference between groups
>
> Weighing these factors, I believe the benefits significantly outweigh the risks, especially with proper monitoring in place."

---

## SLIDE 9: Recommendation (45 seconds)

> "My recommendation is clear: Roll it out.
>
> We have three strong pillars supporting this:
> 1. Proven improvement - a statistically significant 2.6% increase in success
> 2. Higher engagement - players actively use and value the feature more
> 3. Lasting effect - benefits persist, suggesting genuine skill development
>
> However, I recommend implementing monitoring for:
> - Long-term retention metrics at 30 and 90 days
> - Monetization metrics to ensure revenue isn't negatively impacted
> - Game difficulty balance in case adjustments are needed"

---

## SLIDE 10: Questions (30 seconds)

> "To summarize: Our A/B test shows the stronger boost delivers a significant, lasting improvement in player success and engagement.
>
> The data strongly supports rolling out this feature to all players.
>
> Thank you for your attention. I'm happy to take any questions."

## TIPS FOR RECORDING

1. **Pace yourself** - Speak slightly slower than normal conversation
2. **Pause at transitions** - Give viewers time to see each new slide
3. **Point to charts** - When referencing visualizations, use cursor or pointer
4. **Emphasize key numbers** - Slow down when saying "+2.6%" or "p < 0.001"
5. **Sound confident on recommendation** - The data supports it, own the conclusion
